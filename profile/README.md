## About Us

The **World Justice Project (WJP)** is an independent, multidisciplinary, non-profit organization working to advance the rule of law around the world. We believe that the rule of law is the foundation for communities of opportunity and equity — communities that offer sustainable economic development, accountable government, and respect for fundamental rights.

Our work is built on three mutually reinforcing pillars:

- **Research and scholarship** — producing original, independent data on how the rule of law is experienced in everyday life.
- **Convening a global network** — bringing together advocates, practitioners, and policymakers from diverse disciplines and countries.
- **Stimulating, supporting, and connecting locally led programs** — helping translate evidence into action.

## What You'll Find Here

This GitHub organization is home to the open code, datasets, methodologies, and tools produced by WJP teams and collaborators. You can expect to find:

- 📊 **Data and indicators** from the [WJP Rule of Law Index®](https://worldjusticeproject.org/rule-of-law-index/) and related research products.
- 🔬 **Analytical pipelines and notebooks** used to clean, score, and visualize survey and expert data across 140+ countries.
- 🌎 **Regional and thematic projects**, including:
  - [Environmental Governance Indicators for Latin America and the Caribbean](https://worldjusticeproject.org/our-work/research-and-data/environmental-governance-indicators-latin-america-and-caribbean)
  - [WJP EUROVOICES](https://worldjusticeproject.org/our-work/research-and-data/wjp-eurovoices)
  - Country-level reports on access to justice, criminal justice, open government, and the rule of law.
- 🛠️ **Reusable utilities and templates** for working with WJP data and producing comparable, reproducible analyses.

## Flagship Research

The [**WJP Rule of Law Index®**](https://worldjusticeproject.org/rule-of-law-index/) is the world's leading source for original, independent data on the rule of law. It draws on more than 214,000 household and expert surveys to measure how the rule of law is experienced in 142 countries and jurisdictions across eight factors:

1. Constraints on Government Powers
2. Absence of Corruption
3. Open Government
4. Fundamental Rights
5. Order and Security
6. Regulatory Enforcement
7. Civil Justice
8. Criminal Justice

## Contributing

We welcome contributions from researchers, developers, and practitioners working on rule of law, justice, governance, and open data. To get involved:

1. Browse our public repositories and open issues.
2. Read the `CONTRIBUTING.md` guide in the repository you'd like to work on.
3. Open an issue to discuss substantive changes before submitting a pull request.
4. Follow our [Code of Conduct](./CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) in all interactions.

If you are using WJP data or code in your own research, tools, or policy work, we'd love to hear about it — please reach out so we can showcase your work and learn from it.
